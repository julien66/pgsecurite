# Part 1: Conceptual framework. The two paradigms of making a sport safer

We are going to sweep through a series of founding community myths. They are as old as our activity itself and serve as a shared belief and a unifying foundation for everyone who flies. They shape the mindset and the ideas of our community about its favourite sport and the accidents that happen in it. Stepping outside one’s own natural way of thinking is no small matter, and a seasoned paraglider pilot will probably need several readings not to find the paragraphs below disconcerting, even provocative.

This path is nonetheless unavoidable. No one will change the way we fly for good until the basic observations about the causes of accidents are clearly set out and shared. As Thomas Kuhn showed[^1], paradigm shifts are conceptual and scientific before they are practical. Let us make the effort to take an alternative point of view!

Paradoxically, this first part may be easier to read for laypeople and beginners. Beliefs take hold over time, taking root through contact with the community itself and even more directly through the training it provides. All in all, a newcomer acquires the value system and culture of their new discipline fairly quickly. It nevertheless takes time for them to anchor it and to hold the founding myths we are about to discuss as true. It is a long process of acculturation that also depends on the pilot’s background and sporting culture.

[^1]: Thomas Kuhn, 1962: *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*
