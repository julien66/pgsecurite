# Accidents

Paragliding, in the broad sense, brings its share of deaths and injuries. We are a small community, and a number of our friends lose their lives every year. As a tribute, I have named a few of those I met along the way. I will not single out any names, because there have been too many and my grief cannot be ranked. Yet I have lost count of the friends and loved ones. Worse, perhaps, I am surely forgetting some… Shame on me, you might think? Resilience takes whatever paths it must. Here the end justifies the means. What I cannot forget, on the other hand, are the three athletes from our federation’s performance programme lost within the space of a few years. Some of the circumstances were truly vile. Lives and families shattered, trauma in abundance, and great emptiness when evening comes.

We will soon bring out a few figures and talk statistics, but the accident rate of our activity, and of competition at international level, is not heading in the right direction. Despite this reality, which few dispute, our community remains relatively closed and abnormally resilient to the accidents our shared passion produces. Everyone finds their own way and handles their own grief. Most of those close to the victims choose to keep flying. Others stop in silence, and safety seems to barely change from one year to the next. I was the first to think that paragliding competition was a safe activity, and I often even said it was safer than traditional flying, in that it offered a more rigid framework that I considered effective and protective. Today I no longer think our activity is “safe”, neither in competition nor in leisure flying, and through these pages I want to invite us to take a hard look at ourselves in the mirror. First, I will set out the myths and mechanisms behind this surprising resilience of our community. Then I will propose concrete ways out of this status quo, without giving up what lies at the heart of free flight: its freedom. Competition, which I know closely, deserves a work of its own; it will be the subject of a second book.
